0. WHY WE ARE BUILDING THIS 
Novel is scaling fast across 4 brands, 2 plants and 8+ sales channels. At this scale the failure modes are no longer "we 
forgot to order" — they are: 
1. Stock-outs on hero SKUs while dead stock sits at another node. 
2. Quick-commerce fill-rate misses → penalties, delisting, ranking loss. 
3. No batch traceability. A single consumer complaint on a baby SKU with no forward/backward trace is an 
existential risk. Baby care and cosmetics are regulated, litigated categories. 
4. Purchase decisions on WhatsApp and gut feel, not on a net-requirement calculation. 
5. QC as a signature, not a system — no sampling discipline, no NCR trail, no vendor accountability.

3.2 Ingestion pipeline 
Upload (portal / email / API / bulk) 
→ Store original file immutably (S3-equivalent, hash-stamped, never overwritten) 
→ Classify document type 
→ Extract (OCR + layout parsing; native parse for PDFs with text layer; direct parse for XML/JSON e-
invoice) 
→ Normalise (vendor GSTIN → vendor code; item description + HSN → item code via a learned mapping table) 
→ Validate (GSTIN format, tax arithmetic, HSN vs item master, duplicate invoice check) 
→ MATCH against the open PO / GRN / dispatch 
→ Auto-post if clean, else route to the EXCEPTION QUEUE with the reason 
→ Human resolves exception; the resolution trains the mapping table 
 
3.3 Matching rules 
▪ Purchase invoice: match on vendor + PO number + item + qty + rate. 3-way match against PO and GRN accepted 
quantity (post-QC). Tolerances default to 0% on both qty and rate; anything outside blocks and queues. 
▪ Duplicate detection: vendor GSTIN + invoice number + FY is a hard unique key. Reject duplicates loudly. 
▪ Unmatched invoice: parked in the exception queue with an ageing clock. Weekly report of invoices unmatched > 
7 days to SCM Head; > 15 days to AP. 
▪ Item mapping table: vendor's description ↔ our item code, learned from confirmed matches, editable, with 
confidence score. Below a confidence threshold it always goes to a human. 
3.4 What invoice upload CAN and CANNOT do — please read 
It can: capture spend, prices, tax, vendor performance on paper, sales value, channel settlements, ITC reconciliation, 
and give you a complete financial picture with one day's lag. 
It cannot: 
▪ Hold quantity accurately. An invoice says what was billed, not what physically arrived, passed QC, or was put 
away. Quantity must come from a scanned GRN.

▪ Give you batch, MFG date, expiry. These come from the physical receipt, not the invoice. Without them there is 
no traceability, no FEFO, no recall, no QC module — the entire Section 10 collapses. 
▪ Give you quality state. Quarantine vs released is a decision, not a document. 
▪ Support MRP. Auto-PO needs real-time on-hand, in-transit and reserved quantities. A one-day-lagged invoice feed 
cannot drive a reorder point. 
▪ Capture production, consumption, wastage, yield — no invoice exists for these. 
Therefore the operating model is: 
Physical events are transacted live in BOS on the scanner (GRN, putaway, QC, issue, production, pick, dispatch, count). 
Documents are uploaded daily to enrich, value and reconcile those events. 
 
Invoice upload is the financial and reconciliation spine. Scanning is the physical spine. The system needs both; either 
alone gives you a half-system. This is the one point in the whole design I'd push back on hardest — if the plan is 
invoice-upload-only, drop the QC module and MRP from scope, because they cannot work. 
3.5 Exception queue 
A single work-list screen: document, type, source, reason for exception, ageing, assigned owner, action. Bulk actions 
supported. The health of this queue is the health of the data. Put its size on AP's daily dashboard.

The layer the packaged ERP would have given us. Build it first; everything sits on it. 
4.1 Document engine 
▪ Every business event is a document with: type, series, number, date, company, status (Draft / Submitted /

▪ Gapless numbering per type/company/FY, server-generated, never editable

4.2 Stock ledger (the core table) 
stock_ledger_entry:

Applied by default at the ORM/data-access layer to every table: who, what, when, old value, new value, reason.

6.2 Formulas — implement exactly 
Safety stock (demand + lead-time variability): 
SS = Z × √( LT × σd²  +  d̄² × σLT² ) 
 
▪ Z from target service level: 95% → 1.65 | 97% → 1.88 | 98% → 2.05 | 99% → 2.33 
▪ d̄, σd = mean and std dev of daily demand 
▪ LT, σLT = mean and std dev of lead time — computed from actual GRN date minus PO date. Never use the 
vendor's claimed lead time. 
Reorder point: ROP = (d̄ × LT) + SS 
EOQ: EOQ = √( 2 × D × S / H ) — D = annual demand, S = ordering cost per order, H = annual holding cost per 
unit (use 18–22% of unit cost for India: cost of capital + storage + obsolescence) 
Max stock: Max = ROP + EOQ, hard-capped by shelf life: 
Max_allowed = d̄ × (Shelf_life_days × 0.6)      // never hold more than 60% of shelf life 
 
Days of cover: DOC = Closing stock ÷ trailing-28-day average daily run rate 
Order-up-to level (periodic review): S = d̄ × (LT + Review_period) + SS 
6.3 Node-level policy — where most companies fail 
Policy is set per SKU per node, not per SKU: 
▪ Amazon FBA — cover target by FC, respecting IPI and restock limits 
▪ Flipkart FC, Meesho 
▪ Quick-commerce dark stores — 3–7 days cover, replenished 2–3× weekly, very tight 
▪ Plant / CFA — covers the gap and buffers channel volatility 
Network cover is the sum, and it must be visible on one screen. Otherwise you buy stock that already exists 400 km 
away. 
6.4 Redistribution engine 
Daily job: find (SKU, node) pairs where node A is above max while node B is below ROP → suggest an STO with 
cost/benefit (transfer cost vs stock-out cost) → approval → STO document with e-way bill. 
6.5 Alerts 
Below ROP | below SS | projected stock-out inside the lead-time window | above max or cover > 120 days | zero stock 
on an active A-class SKU (escalate to AP) | near expiry at 6/4/3/2 months | no movement 90/180 days.
